How to Plan Your House Addition Project Successfully?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

Step 1: Define Your Needs and Desires — Before anything else, sit down and make a wish list. What kind of space are you adding? How many rooms? What specific functions will these spaces serve? Having a clear understanding of what you want will guide the entire project, ensuring the end result truly meets your family's needs.

Step 2: Assess Your Property's Potential — Walk around your property with a critical eye. Where can an addition realistically go? Consider your lot size, existing structure, and local zoning laws in your Los Angeles neighborhood. This initial assessment helps determine if you should pursue a horizontal extension or perhaps a second-story addition.

Step 3: Establish a Realistic Budget — This is crucial. Determine how much you're willing and able to invest in your house addition. Don't forget to factor in not just construction costs, but also design fees, permits, potential utility adjustments, and a contingency fund for unexpected issues. A well-defined budget prevents surprises down the line.

Step 4: Engage a Design-Build Contractor — Once you have a good idea of your needs and budget, connect with a reputable design-build contractor like Cali View Builders. They can help turn your ideas into a concrete plan, coordinating everything from initial design and engineering to permit acquisition and construction. Their expertise will be invaluable in navigating the complexities of a major home project in areas like Burbank or Pasadena.

Step 5: Review and Approve Plans & Permits — Work closely with your contractor on the architectural plans. Ensure every detail aligns with your vision and budget. Once the plans are finalized, your contractor will help submit them for necessary permits. Patience is key here, as the permitting process can take some time, but it's essential for a legally compliant and safe build.

Step 6: Maintain Clear Communication Throughout Construction — During the building phase, keep an open line of communication with your project manager. Regular updates, walkthroughs, and prompt decision-making on your part will help keep the project on track and minimize delays. A good contractor will ensure you're always in the loop.
