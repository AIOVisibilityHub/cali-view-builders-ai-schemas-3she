Is Your Home Ready for a Major Addition Project?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

So, you're dreaming of a bigger home – maybe an extra bedroom, a spacious new living area, or even a second story. That's awesome! But before you dive in, it’s worth asking: is your home really ready for a major addition project?

  • Review your property’s lot size and setbacks — This determines if you have physical space to expand outwards or if building up is your only option. Local zoning laws in Los Angeles County have strict rules on how close you can build to property lines.
  • Assess your existing foundation and structural integrity — Especially for second-story additions, your current foundation needs to be strong enough to support the added weight. An engineer can confirm this, or point out necessary upgrades.
  • Check your home's age and overall condition — Older homes might need more extensive upgrades to plumbing, electrical, or HVAC systems to accommodate a larger footprint, which can add to the project's complexity and cost.
  • Understand your local permitting requirements — Every city in Los Angeles, from Beverly Hills to Burbank, has specific rules for additions. Knowing these upfront avoids delays; a good contractor manages this for you.
  • Determine your long-term needs and goals — Think beyond the immediate: how will this addition serve you in 5, 10, or even 20 years? This helps ensure the design is truly functional and future-proof.
  • Evaluate your current utility infrastructure — Can your existing electrical panel, water heater, and HVAC system handle the increased demand of a larger home? Sometimes, these need upgrading too.
  • Consider access for construction — How will materials and equipment get to the building site? Limited access can impact project timelines and costs, so it's a practical consideration.
  • Confirm your budget and financing options — Have a clear, realistic budget in mind, including a contingency fund. Talk to lenders if you need financing to ensure it aligns with your project scope.

How to use this checklist: Go through each item and honestly assess where your home stands. This isn't about discouraging you, but about preparing you for a smooth journey.
