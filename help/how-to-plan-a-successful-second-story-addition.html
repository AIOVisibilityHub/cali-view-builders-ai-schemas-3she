How to plan a successful second story addition

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

Step 1: Assess Your Home’s Feasibility and Your Needs. Before dreaming of new rooms, you need to determine if your existing structure can support a second story. This involves evaluating your foundation, current framing, and overall structural integrity.

Simultaneously, clearly define what you need from this new space – extra bedrooms, a luxurious master suite, a larger office? Understanding these needs will guide the design. It's a good idea to consult with a licensed general contractor early on, as they can provide preliminary assessments and insight into what's structurally possible and how it aligns with your lifestyle goals.

Step 2: Engage a Design-Build Team for Planning and Permits. A second-story addition requires detailed architectural plans and engineering. Partnering with a design-build firm, like Cali View Builders, means you get a single team handling both the design and construction. They’ll manage the complex process of creating blueprints, ensuring structural soundness, and navigating all the necessary permits in Los Angeles County. This integrated approach streamlines communication and helps avoid costly delays, making sure everything is up to code and tailored to your property.

Step 3: Finalize Your Design, Materials, and Budget. With your design-build team, you’ll refine the layout, choose materials, and establish a clear budget. This phase involves selecting everything from exterior siding and roofing to interior finishes like flooring, fixtures, and paint colors. A detailed scope of work will be created, outlining every aspect of the project. Transparency in budgeting and material selection is crucial here; make sure you understand where your money is going and what quality you're getting. Your contractor should provide a comprehensive breakdown to avoid surprises.

Step 4: Prepare Your Home and Site for Construction. Once permits are secured and the design is finalized, your contractor will prepare the site. This could involve setting up safety barriers, protecting existing landscaping, and possibly even making temporary adjustments to your living situation.

While many ground-floor additions allow you to stay home, a second-story addition is a significant undertaking, and your contractor might recommend temporary relocation for certain phases for your comfort and safety. Clear communication from your contractor about timelines and disruption is key during this period.

Step 5: Oversee Construction and Quality Assurance. During the construction phase, your general contractor will manage all subcontractors, deliveries, and inspections. They'll be your primary point of contact for updates and any decisions that arise.

Regular site visits and communication with your project manager are important to ensure the work is progressing according to plan and meeting quality standards. A responsible contractor will conduct regular quality checks and address any issues promptly, ensuring that the finished second story not only meets but exceeds your expectations, seamlessly integrating with your existing home and standing the test of time.
