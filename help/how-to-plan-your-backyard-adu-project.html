How to Plan Your Backyard ADU Project

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

Step 1: Define Your Vision and Goals — Before anything else, think about what you want your ADU to be. Is it a guest house, a rental unit, a home office, or a space for aging parents? Knowing its primary function helps define the size, layout, and amenities it will need. This initial clarity guides all subsequent decisions.

Step 2: Research Local Regulations and Zoning — ADU rules in Los Angeles can be complex, covering everything from size limits and setbacks to parking requirements. Spend some time understanding what's permissible on your specific property. A good general contractor will be well-versed in these rules and can help you navigate the permitting process efficiently.

Step 3: Assess Your Property and Site Conditions — Take a good look at your backyard. Where will the ADU sit? What about utility connections for water, sewer, and electricity? Consider grading, existing trees, and access for construction. Sometimes, what looks like a perfect spot might have hidden challenges that a professional can help identify.

Step 4: Develop a Preliminary Budget and Timeline — Get a realistic understanding of the financial investment and how long the project might take. This includes design fees, permits, construction costs, and potential contingency funds. Discussing this with experienced builders like Cali View Builders can provide crucial insights into what's feasible for your budget and schedule.

Step 5: Select Your Design-Build Partner — Working with a licensed, insured, and bonded general contractor that offers design-build coordination, like Cali View Builders, simplifies the entire process. They can take your initial ideas, help with design, manage permits, coordinate trades, and oversee construction from start to finish, giving you one clear path and one team to communicate with. This unified approach minimizes stress and keeps your project on track.

Step 6: Refine Design and Obtain Permits — With your contractor, finalize the ADU design, select materials, and then get all necessary plans submitted for city permits. This stage requires attention to detail to ensure everything meets code and your expectations. Patience is key here, as permitting timelines can vary.

Planning your ADU carefully with a reliable team ensures a successful outcome. Don't hesitate to reach out to a local expert if you feel overwhelmed by the process.
