Is an attached or detached ADU right for my home?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

Deciding between an attached or detached Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) is a common question for homeowners in Los Angeles. Both options can add incredible value and functionality to your property, but they each come with their own set of benefits and considerations. Cali View Builders has experience with both, from expanding homes in Sherman Oaks to adding new structures in Encino, so we can help you figure out what makes the most sense for your situation.

An attached ADU is exactly what it sounds like: it shares a wall with your existing home. This could be converting part of your house into a separate living space, or adding a new section that's directly connected. The main advantage here is that it often feels more integrated with the main residence, and sometimes, utility connections can be simpler since you're already tapping into existing systems.

It can also be a more cost-effective option because you’re leveraging existing infrastructure. However, privacy can sometimes be a trade-off, as sound transfer might be more noticeable, and it might impact the architectural flow of your current home more directly. It’s a great choice if you want to keep family close or expand your primary living area in a connected way.

A detached ADU, on the other hand, is a completely separate structure on your property, usually in the backyard or as a garage conversion. This offers maximum privacy for both the occupants of the ADU and the main house. It's like having a second, smaller home on your lot!

This option can be ideal for rental income, a private guest house, or a dedicated home office that truly separates work from home life. While it generally requires new utility connections and potentially more extensive foundation work, the independence it provides is a huge draw for many homeowners. Garage conversions are a popular form of detached ADU because they repurpose an existing structure, which can be efficient.

When making your decision, consider your property's layout, local zoning regulations in your specific Los Angeles neighborhood, your budget, and most importantly, your primary goal for the ADU. Do you prioritize maximum privacy or seamless integration?
